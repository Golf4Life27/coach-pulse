## What the law requires (ORC 5301.95)

- **Who is a "wholesaler":** anyone who, for compensation, enters a purchase
  contract as grantee and assigns/novates it to another — or acts as grantor
  without holding legal title. (Blood relatives and affiliated entities exempt.)
- **Before any binding contract:** a **standalone written disclosure** — separate
  from the purchase contract, **boldface, ≥ 12-point font** — must be **signed
  and dated by BOTH the record owner and the wholesaler**. No signed disclosure
  → the wholesaler may not enter the contract at all.
- **Required content:** identifies the presenter as a wholesaler (as defined in
  5301.95); parties are entitled to legal/professional advice; the wholesaler
  acts for itself, not the owner; the wholesaler may **assign the contract for
  profit without the owner's consent**; the agreed purchase price **may be below
  market value**; failure to present/complete the form is an **unfair or
  deceptive act or practice**.
- **Remedies for non-compliance:** the owner may **cancel any time before close
  of escrow without penalty**; any earnest money is disbursed **to the owner**
  within 30 days; Ohio consumer-protection (CSPA) enforcement applies.
- **(C)(2): nothing in the section can be modified or waived** by any oral or
  written agreement. There is no contracting around this.
- Secondary sources indicate **double closings (taking title first) are outside
  the statute** — assignment is the regulated act. Verify with counsel before
  relying on this as a workaround.

## AKB operating rules for Ohio deals (binding, per operator directive 2026-08-16)

1. **Sequence is disclosure → signatures (both parties) → THEN contract.**
   Never simultaneous, never combined into the purchase agreement.
2. The disclosure is **operator-signed and seller-signed** before any OH
   purchase agreement goes out for signature. Manual/DocuSign both fine; the
   form must render in **bold ≥ 12pt**.
3. **EMD exposure rule:** until the signed disclosure is in hand, no earnest
   money moves on an OH deal — a defective disclosure converts EMD into the
   owner's money.
4. Applies to **assignment-model deals including creative/seller-finance
   contracts** we intend to assign (Hadley class). If a deal will be **double
   closed** instead, flag it to counsel; do not self-certify the exemption.
5. **Gate enforcement (docketed, not yet built):** when the contract path
   (Gate 4 / pa_document) wires up, add a PC-17 checklist item mirroring PC-16
   (TN): OH deal → `sb155_disclosure_signed` attestation required before
   `pre_contract` can pass.

**OHIO WHOLESALER DISCLOSURE — REQUIRED BY OHIO REVISED CODE § 5301.95**

**Property address:** ______________________________________________

**Record owner(s):** ______________________________________________

**Wholesaler (buyer):** ____________________________________________

**Date presented to owner:** _______________________________________

**Ohio law requires a wholesaler acting as a grantee, before entering into a
contract or agreement that conveys an interest in residential real property,
to provide certain information to the record owner in a conspicuous manner
printed in boldface type in a font size not less than twelve points. Failure
by a wholesaler to present or complete this form is an unfair or deceptive
act or practice. Any person who enters into an agreement that conveys an
interest in residential real property to a wholesaler acting as a grantee
without receiving this disclosure has a cause of action against the
wholesaler. A wholesaler acting as a grantee is prohibited from entering
into a binding contract to acquire an interest in residential real property
unless this statement is signed and dated by the record owner of the
property.**

**The owner acknowledges that the person presenting this document is a
wholesaler, as defined by section 5301.95 of the Revised Code, and that all
buyers and sellers of real estate are entitled to seek legal or professional
advice before entering into any agreement or contract regarding the purchase
or sale of property, including an agreement with a wholesaler. A wholesaler
is acting on the wholesaler's own behalf and does not represent the owner in
this transaction. A wholesaler enters assignable contracts with owners and
seeks to sell or assign the wholesaler's interest for a profit. The
wholesaler may assign the wholesaler's interest in the purchase contract to
a third party without the owner's consent before closing. The wholesaler may
charge a fee to the third-party buyer separately for profit. The agreed
purchase price between the owner and wholesaler may be below market value
and is conveyed voluntarily.**

### Statutory context printed on/behind the form (not part of the (B)(1) text)

- ORC 5301.95(B)(2): the wholesaler shall not enter a binding contract until
  **both** the wholesaler and record owner sign and date this statement.
- ORC 5301.95(C)(1): absent proper disclosure, the owner may cancel any time
  before close of escrow without penalty, and the escrow/closing agent shall
  disburse any earnest money paid by the wholesaler **to the owner** within
  30 days of cancellation.
- ORC 5301.95(C)(2): no provision of the section can be modified or waived by
  any oral or written agreement — any such term is **void ab initio**.
- ORC 5301.95(C)(3): violations are unfair/deceptive acts under ORC 1345.02,
  with consumer remedies per 1345.09 and Ohio Attorney General enforcement.

## Georgia note (Carolyn Cir class — NOT SB 155)

Georgia has no SB 155-equivalent wholesaler disclosure statute as of this
writing. Unlicensed wholesaling stays lawful in GA when marketing the
**contract interest** (never the property itself) — keep dispo marketing
language contract-framed on GA deals. Re-verify at contract time.
